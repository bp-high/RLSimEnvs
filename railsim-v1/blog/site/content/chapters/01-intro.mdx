## The job

Between Leeuwarden and Groningen, in the north of the Netherlands, runs 55 km of mostly single track. Trains going
opposite ways can only pass each other at stations with two tracks. Eight trains an hour share it. When something
goes wrong (a broken-down train, a collision at a level crossing, a signal failure) a dispatcher has a few minutes to
re-plan the next three hours: hold this train at that station, turn that one back early, cancel another.

[RailSimEnv](https://huggingface.co/spaces/bpHigh/RailSimEnv) [@railsimenv] turns real days from 2025 into that job.
It is an OpenEnv [@openenv] environment rebuilt from the public record of every train on the line [@rdt2025], and it
grades every plan against the best plan possible, proven by a solver. We took Thinking Machines' newest small model,
**Inkling 2 Small**, measured it, and fine-tuned it on the environment with reinforcement learning through the Tinker
API [@tinker2026]. This is what happened, in plain terms, and what we noticed along the way.

<Note variant="info" emoji="🚆" title="In one paragraph">
  Inkling 2 Small started at <b>0.119</b> on the 45 hard evaluation tasks, below a simple rule-based dispatcher (0.180).
  Reinforcement learning on other days of the same line taught it to stop running out of words: <b>0.186</b>. Giving it
  four times the room to think did more than training: <b>0.259</b> before any training, and <b>0.316</b> after a second
  round of reinforcement learning, close to GLM-5.3-Flash (0.360) with less room than any of the larger models had.
  The last gain is promising but not yet settled.
</Note>
