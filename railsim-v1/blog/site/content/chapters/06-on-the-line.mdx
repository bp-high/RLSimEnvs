## Watching it on the line

Averages hide what a dispatcher actually does. Here are three tasks, before and after the second round of training,
on the environment's train graph (time across, the line from Leeuwarden down to Groningen) and in its 3D replay.
Every plan below is the one the model submitted, replayed exactly as it was graded; all of them can be replayed on
the [RailSimEnv Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/bpHigh/RailSimEnv), first tab.

### 13 March 2025, 07:01: a broken-down train

A real breakdown closed Leeuwarden - Hurdegaryp from 07:01 to 08:00, with 26 trains to plan.

The difference is a decision a human dispatcher would recognise: when a section is closed for an hour, do not queue
trains in front of it, turn them back where they can be turned and keep the rest of the timetable intact. The base
plan's queue does not hurt during the closure; it hurts two and a half hours later, when the delayed trains collide
with the timetable coming the other way.

### 31 May 2025, 09:00: where training made it worse

Not every task improved. A defective track closed Leeuwarden - Hurdegaryp from 09:30 to 09:56, a short closure.

<Wide>
<Stack layout="2-column" gap="medium">
  <Image src={g0531b} alt="Train graph of the base model's plan for 31 May: four short-turns" caption="<b>Before RL: 0.756.</b> The base model short-turns four trains: 246 stop-minutes against an optimum of 226." zoomable />
  <Image src={g0531r} alt="Train graph of the fine-tuned model's plan for 31 May: every train held, none turned" caption="<b>After RL: 0.200.</b> The fine-tuned model holds every train through the closure and turns none back: legal, but 708 stop-minutes of delay." zoomable />
</Stack>
</Wide>

<Note variant="neutral" emoji="🔍" title="What this shows">
  On a 26-minute closure, waiting is a reasonable instinct; the optimum mixes a few short-turns with short holds. The
  fine-tuned model does not yet weigh the closure's length well: it learned that turning back is often right on long
  closures, and here it swung the other way. One rollout per task makes single tasks noisy, which is why the paired
  average over all 45 is the number to trust.
</Note>
